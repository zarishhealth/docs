Use this guide to find information and understand what the organization has decided. You do not need to write code to read or suggest changes to these documents.

## 1. Pick your goal

- **Learn the basics:** read the [architecture overview](../architecture/index.md) and [glossary](../glossary/index.md).
- **Understand the repositories:** use the [repository map](../architecture/repository-map.md).
- **Check a naming or content rule:** see the [reference pages](../reference/naming.md) and [content model](../reference/content-model.md).
- **Suggest a correction:** follow the [contributing guide](../guides/contributing.md).

## 2. Check whether a statement is a proposal

The organization-wide blueprint is marked **proposed**. Its repository catalog and technical design are a target for review, not an inventory of deployed systems. Look for a page's `status` before acting on instructions that could affect a real deployment.

## 3. Understand the organization map

The four current repositories serve different roles. The exact purpose of `builder` should be confirmed in that repository's own README. Other repositories in the blueprint are future proposals unless the organization has since created them.

## 4. Keep examples safe

Do not use real patient, beneficiary, staff, or account data in documentation. Use fictional, clearly synthetic examples. Never put secrets or personal data in a URL, screenshot, log, or diagram.

## 5. Know the limits

These documents are not clinical advice, legal advice, an operational runbook for a live health service, or evidence of regulatory compliance. Confirm current requirements with the responsible technical, clinical, privacy, and legal reviewers before implementation.
